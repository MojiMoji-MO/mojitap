How to Find an Emoji When You Don't Know Its Name

Written by Bavum, Builder and Editor at Mojitap · Published July 25, 2026 · Based on hands-on emoji-tool development and official platform references · About 10 minutes

You remember what an emoji looks like, but not what it is called. Maybe it is the face with a single tear, the hand making a small heart, the symbol that looks like a burst of light, or an object you saw in someone else's message. Searching becomes frustrating when the word in your head is different from the name used by the keyboard.

The reliable solution is to stop looking for one perfect name. Search from several directions: appearance, action, subject, emotion, color, and context. Emoji search systems often use short names and related keywords, so a broad description can work even when you do not know the official label.

The fastest search method

  1. Describe the main idea with one simple word, such as happy, warning, food, or travel.
  2. Try one visible feature or action, such as tear, point, sparkle, or hug.
  3. Replace the word with a synonym if the first search fails. For example, try celebrate, party, and congratulations.
  4. Browse the nearest category and inspect neighboring emojis. Similar characters are commonly grouped together.
  5. Copy the likely result into a full sentence and check whether its tone and meaning fit before sending it.

1. Why emoji search sometimes feels harder than it should

An emoji can have several useful descriptions. A person may remember a symbol as “happy tears,” while a keyboard may associate it with words such as “joy,” “laugh,” “face,” or “tear.” A hand gesture may be remembered by its social meaning, while the search system may use the physical action in its name.

Unicode's Common Locale Data Repository, usually called CLDR, maintains short names and search keywords for emoji characters and sequences. Software platforms can use that information as part of their localized search experience, but the exact results may still differ by operating system, keyboard, language, and software version. That is why a term that works on one phone may not return the same list on another.

Do not treat an online nickname as the official name. Communities often invent memorable labels for emojis. Those labels can help people communicate, but they may not be recognized by a device search box. When a nickname fails, describe the visible object or action instead.

2. Search by meaning, appearance, action, and subject

Search the emotion

For faces, begin with a broad feeling: happy, sad, angry, tired, confused, or relieved.

Search the visible feature

Describe what you can see: tear, sweat, halo, mask, glasses, or tongue.

Search the action

For people and hands, try a verb: wave, clap, point, shrug, run, or dance.

Search the object or topic

For things, places, and symbols, name the subject: coffee, train, phone, money, weather, or music.

Search the color or shape

Color is useful for hearts, circles, squares, flags, and decorative symbols. Try combinations such as blue heart, red circle, or white flag.

Search the situation

When you know what the message is for, search the occasion: birthday, wedding, work, school, vacation, or emergency.

3. Use synonyms instead of repeating the same failed search

Re-entering the same word rarely improves the result. Move sideways to a related word. If you search for “success” and see nothing useful, try “win,” “award,” “trophy,” “check,” “celebrate,” or “goal.” If “love” is too broad, try “heart,” “affection,” “couple,” “kiss,” or a specific color.

The best synonym depends on what you remember. Use a physical word when you remember the image, an emotional word when you remember the tone, and a situational word when you remember where it was used.

4. Browse the category and inspect nearby emojis

Search is not always the fastest route. If you know the general type, open the relevant category: smileys, people, animals, food, travel, activities, objects, symbols, or flags. Standard emoji lists usually place related characters near one another, so finding one similar item can lead you to the exact one.

This method is particularly effective for facial expressions, hand gestures, transportation, sports, and colored symbols. It also helps when the keyboard's search language does not match the language you used to describe the emoji.

5. Search from the sentence instead of the emoji

Sometimes you do not remember a specific emoji at all. You only know the sentence needs a visual cue. In that case, write the full message first. The surrounding words reveal whether you need celebration, caution, gratitude, travel, food, work, affection, or another topic.

Example

Draft sentence: “The final version is ready for review.”

Possible searches: ready, complete, check, document, or review.

A clear result might be: “The final version is ready for review ✅” The words remain complete, and the emoji acts as a visual confirmation rather than replacing essential information.

6. How to search on Windows, Mac, iPhone, and Android

Windows

Place the cursor in a text field and press Windows key + . to open the emoji panel. Microsoft documents that you can select an emoji or continue typing to search the available list. If the panel does not open, click inside an editable field before trying again.

Mac

Open the Character Viewer from the app's Edit menu or use the current keyboard shortcut configured on the Mac. Apple's Character Viewer supports searching and inserting emoji, symbols, and other characters. Search terms and shortcut behavior can vary with macOS version and keyboard settings.

iPhone

Switch to the emoji keyboard and use the Search Emoji field when it is available. If the emoji keyboard is missing, add it in the iPhone keyboard settings. Newer characters may require a current iOS version before they display correctly.

Android

Open the emoji section of the installed keyboard. Gboard and other keyboards can differ by device, language, and version, so the search button may appear in a toolbar or inside the emoji panel. If your keyboard offers no useful search, browse a category or use a web-based emoji list.

7. Check whether you found an emoji, symbol, sticker, or image

Modern keyboards may place standard emoji, stickers, GIFs, avatars, and generated images in nearby tabs. These items do not behave the same way. A standard Unicode emoji usually travels with text and can be pasted into many apps. A sticker or GIF is an image and may be sent as a separate attachment.

Before using the result in a document, form, email subject, or web page, paste it into a plain-text field. If it behaves like one text character, it is likely a standard emoji or symbol. If it appears as a large graphic, preview card, or attachment, return to the standard emoji tab.

8. What to do when the emoji appears as a box or is missing

An empty box, replacement mark, or broken-looking sequence usually means the current operating system, font, browser, or app does not support that character yet. Unicode can define an emoji before every platform ships artwork for it.

  1. Update the operating system, browser, and messaging app.
  2. Test the emoji in another current app or device.
  3. Choose an older, widely supported alternative when compatibility matters.
  4. Keep important meaning in words so a missing symbol does not break the message.

9. A practical checklist before you send

  • Does the full sentence still make sense without the emoji?
  • Is the symbol appropriate for the relationship and situation?
  • Could the same emoji be interpreted differently in another culture or community?
  • Is it a standard text emoji rather than a sticker or image?
  • Will the recipient's device probably support it?
  • Have you avoided a long decorative sequence that makes the message harder to read?

Frequently asked questions

Can I find an emoji from a picture?

A built-in keyboard may not accept an image as a search query. Start by listing visible details: face or object, color, action, number of people, surrounding symbols, and likely category. Use those words in the keyboard or an emoji browser.

Why does the same search show different emojis on two devices?

Platforms may use different software versions, languages, localized keywords, and search-ranking rules. Their artwork also differs even when the underlying character is the same.

Is there one official name for every emoji?

Unicode data includes standardized names and annotations, but people often use informal names in conversation. Search keywords and local translations can provide several ways to find the same character.

What is the best word to search when I only know the meaning?

Start with the message purpose: celebration, warning, gratitude, travel, work, love, food, weather, or another broad topic. Then narrow the results with an action, object, color, or visible feature.
