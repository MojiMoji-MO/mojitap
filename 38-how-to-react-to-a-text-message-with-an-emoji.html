How to React to a Text Message with an Emoji on iPhone and Android

Written by Bavum, Builder and Editor at Mojitap · Published July 31, 2026 · Reviewed with current Apple and Google documentation · About 10 minutes

An emoji reaction attaches a small symbol to a specific message, so you can acknowledge it without sending a separate reply. On iPhone, Apple calls these reactions Tapbacks. In Google Messages on Android, you add a reaction by touching and holding the message.

The basic action is simple, but the result can vary between iMessage, RCS, SMS, and MMS. A reaction that looks attached on your phone may appear as descriptive text on an older app or unsupported device. Start with the platform steps below, then use the troubleshooting section only when the reaction does not appear as expected.

The quickest way to add a reaction

On iPhone: Open Messages, double-tap or touch and hold a message, then choose a suggested Tapback. To use another emoji, swipe through the suggestions or open the emoji choices.

On Android with Google Messages: Touch and hold the message, then tap the emoji reaction you want. Touch and hold it again to change the reaction, or tap the highlighted reaction to remove your own reaction.

React to a text on iPhone

  1. Open the Messages app.
  2. Open the conversation containing the message.
  3. Double-tap the message, or touch and hold it.
  4. Tap one of the suggested Tapbacks.
  5. For a different emoji, swipe left through suggestions or tap the emoji button.
  6. Tap outside the menu when you are finished.

React to a text on Android

  1. Open Google Messages.
  2. Open the conversation containing the message.
  3. Touch and hold the message.
  4. Choose an emoji reaction from the row that appears.
  5. Touch and hold the message again if you want to change it.
  6. Tap the highlighted reaction to remove your own reaction.

A reaction is different from replying with an emoji

A reaction is attached to an existing message. Sending an emoji from the keyboard creates a new message bubble. This distinction matters when you want to acknowledge a specific sentence in a busy group chat.

Reaction A small thumbs-up appears on the message “The meeting starts at 3.” Everyone can see which message the acknowledgment belongs to.
Separate emoji reply A new thumbs-up message appears below the conversation. In a fast thread, it may be less clear which earlier message it refers to.

Use a reaction for a quick acknowledgment. Use a written reply when the message needs confirmation, a decision, a correction, or a time-sensitive answer. A heart or thumbs-up can be interpreted differently by different people, so important meaning should remain in words.

How to change or remove an emoji reaction

On iPhone

Double-tap or touch and hold the message again. Select a different Tapback to replace the current one. To remove your reaction, select the same highlighted Tapback again. Apple allows one Tapback from each participant on a message, while stickers can be placed separately.

On Android with Google Messages

Touch and hold the message you reacted to. Choose another emoji to replace your reaction. To remove it, tap the reaction that is already highlighted. You can change or remove only your own reaction.

Find out who reacted in a group chat

Tap the group of reactions attached to the message. The app can show which participant selected each reaction. This is more reliable than guessing from the number of symbols in a busy conversation.

Why a reaction sometimes appears as text

A reaction requires both sides of the conversation to understand how the reaction is attached to the original message. When the receiving phone, app, carrier service, or message type does not support the same behavior, the reaction may be converted into text such as a description of what someone liked or emphasized.

Do not judge the connection by bubble color alone. On iPhone, blue bubbles use iMessage. Green bubbles can use RCS, SMS, or MMS. RCS can support richer messaging features, while SMS and MMS have more limited reaction behavior. Availability also depends on the carrier, region, device, and messaging app.

In Google Messages, Google currently documents SMS and MMS reactions for users in the United States, France, Canada, and Australia. Outside supported conditions, RCS may be required for a native reaction. The exact availability can change, so test the conversation rather than assuming every green-bubble or cross-platform chat behaves the same.

What to do when reactions do not appear

  1. Confirm you are using Apple Messages or Google Messages, not a different app with separate controls.
  2. Update the phone's operating system and the messaging app.
  3. Restart the app, then restart the phone if the interface remains unresponsive.
  4. Try reacting to a recent plain-text message rather than an old attachment.
  5. Check whether the conversation uses iMessage, RCS, SMS, or MMS.
  6. On Android, confirm that Google Messages is connected to the device and RCS is active when available.
  7. On iPhone, check Settings > Apps > Messages if iMessage or RCS features are missing.
  8. Ask the other participant to update their messaging app when only they see a text description.
  9. Use a normal reply instead of a reaction when compatibility remains uncertain.

One-app test: Reactions in WhatsApp, Instagram, Slack, Discord, and other apps follow those apps' own rules. A reaction working in Apple Messages or Google Messages does not prove it will work in every service, and changing a system message setting may not affect an app-specific reaction picker.

Choose reactions that remain clear

A thumbs-up can mean “I saw this,” “I agree,” or “the task is complete.” A heart can mean affection, appreciation, or support. Laughter can be friendly in one conversation and dismissive in another. The symbol is fast, but it is not a complete sentence.

Clear enough for a reaction “The package arrived.” A thumbs-up can simply acknowledge the update.
Better with words “Can you approve the final price today?” Reply “Approved” or explain what is still needed instead of relying only on a reaction.

For workplace, healthcare, travel, payment, safety, or deadline-related messages, keep the decision in text. A reaction can support the reply, but it should not carry the only record of an important instruction or agreement.

Accessibility and group-chat checks

Screen readers may announce an emoji reaction by its accessible name, which can be more verbose than the small symbol appears visually. A row of repeated reactions can also interrupt the reading flow. Use reactions sparingly when the conversation already contains complex information.

In a group conversation, tap the reaction summary to confirm who reacted instead of inferring agreement from the total count. One participant may be acknowledging receipt while another is expressing approval. A short follow-up message is safer when individual intent matters.

Do not treat a reaction as a guaranteed receipt. A reaction can fail to synchronize, be removed, or display differently on another device. Use the messaging service's delivery or read status, followed by a written confirmation, when proof of receipt is important.
