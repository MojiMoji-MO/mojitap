Accessible Emoji Use for Screen Readers and Clear Writing

Written by Bavum, Builder and Editor at Mojitap · Originally published June 2026 · Updated August 7, 2026

Keep essential information in words and use emojis as support. Screen readers can announce Unicode emoji by their defined names, which may not match the meaning you intended. Repetition, symbol-only labels, and decorative emoji inside controls can make content harder to understand.

Accessibility rule

Remove the emoji mentally. If an instruction, status, label, or warning disappears, add the missing meaning in text.

Understand what may be announced

Unicode emojis have predefined character names. A screen reader may announce that name rather than your conversational interpretation. The W3C technique for emojis and emoticons recommends providing text alternatives that serve the same purpose when the default representation is insufficient.

Limit repeated emoji and punctuation

A visual row may look like emphasis, but assistive technology may announce every character. Put the meaningful heading in words and use no more decoration than the message needs.

Label links, buttons, and form controls

A magnifying glass, paper plane, or trash can may be familiar visually, but controls need an accessible name. In web content, use visible text or an appropriate programmatic label. Do not assume the emoji's Unicode name describes the action correctly.

Distinguish Unicode emoji from images and stickers

A standard emoji is text. A custom emoji, sticker, reaction image, or screenshot may be an image. Image-based content needs an alternative that communicates its purpose when that purpose is not already stated nearby.

Review a message without claiming a device test

  1. Read the sentence aloud, including the likely emoji name.
  2. Remove repeated symbols and check whether the rhythm improves.
  3. Confirm that every action and status is written in words.
  4. Check links and controls for descriptive labels.
  5. Use an actual screen reader test when available, but do not claim one was performed unless it was.
