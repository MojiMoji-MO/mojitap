Gmail Emoji Reactions: How to Add, Remove, and Fix Them

Written by Bavum, Builder and Editor at Mojitap · Published August 16, 2026 · Reviewed with current Google Gmail and Google Workspace documentation · About 9 minutes

Gmail emoji reactions let you respond to an email without writing a normal reply. Open the message, choose Add emoji reaction, and select an emoji. If you react by mistake, Gmail lets you use Undo for a short period after sending the reaction. If the reaction button is missing, the cause may be the account, the message type, the number of recipients, a Workspace administrator setting, or the email app you are using.

The most useful way to troubleshoot Gmail reactions is to separate three different things: an emoji typed inside an email, a Gmail reaction attached to a message, and a reaction that arrives as a separate notification email. They look related, but they do not behave the same way.

The short answer

What changed for Gmail emoji reactions in 2026?

Gmail reactions are no longer limited to the earlier personal-account rollout. Google Workspace expanded emoji reactions to Workspace customers and announced that the feature would be enabled by default starting February 9, 2026 unless an organization had disabled or changed the setting.

That matters when troubleshooting a work or school account. If reactions appear for one person but not another, do not assume the Gmail app is broken. A Workspace administrator can turn the feature off for users, and users can still receive reactions even when their own ability to send them is disabled.

Useful distinction: a missing reaction button on a managed account can be an administrator setting, while a missing button on one specific email can be caused by the message itself. Check both before reinstalling Gmail or changing unrelated settings.

How to add a Gmail emoji reaction

On Gmail in a web browser

  1. Open Gmail and open the message you want to react to.
  2. Find Add emoji reaction near the Reply control or below the message.
  3. Select an emoji.
  4. The reaction appears at the bottom of the email.
  5. To see who used a reaction, hover over the reaction chip.

If someone already reacted with the emoji you want, Gmail lets you reuse the existing reaction chip instead of opening the picker again.

On the Gmail mobile app

  1. Open the message in the Gmail app.
  2. Tap Add emoji reaction.
  3. Choose a visible emoji or open the larger emoji selection.
  4. The reaction appears under the message.
  5. Touch and hold a reaction to check who used it.

Google describes Gmail reactions as best experienced in Gmail on the web and in the Gmail mobile apps. If you open the same mailbox through a third-party email app, the reaction feature can behave differently or be unavailable.

Can you remove a Gmail emoji reaction after sending it?

Gmail documents one removal method: use Undo immediately after adding the reaction. The available time is tied to your Gmail Undo Send setting, which can be set from 5 to 30 seconds.

If the Undo message is already gone: Google's current Gmail help page does not document a later delete button for an already-sent emoji reaction. Do not keep clicking the reaction or send another reaction expecting the first one to disappear.

If the reaction was accidental and the undo window has passed, the clearest recovery is usually a short written reply when context matters.

Example:
“Sorry — that reaction was accidental. I meant to acknowledge the update.”

A written correction is especially useful when the reaction could be misunderstood, such as a work message, approval request, complaint, or sensitive conversation.

Why the Gmail emoji reaction button is missing

Before changing app settings, match your situation to the restriction. Gmail intentionally prevents reactions in several cases.

What you see Likely reason What to do next
No reaction option on a work or school account Your Google Workspace administrator may have disabled emoji reactions Use a normal reply or ask the administrator whether the feature is allowed
No reaction option on a group message The message was sent to a group email list or includes a Google Group Reply normally instead of troubleshooting the emoji picker
No reaction option on a large-recipient email The message was sent to more than 20 recipients Use Reply or Reply all only when appropriate
You are a BCC recipient Gmail does not allow a reaction in this situation Send a normal reply only if it is appropriate to reveal your address
Message came from an alias or has a custom reply-to address The message does not meet Gmail reaction conditions Use a written reply
Message uses client-side encryption Emoji reactions are unavailable for that encrypted message Respond using the supported encrypted-email workflow
You opened Gmail mail in Outlook or Apple Mail The Gmail reaction control is not available in that third-party provider Open the message in Gmail if you need the Gmail reaction feature
You have reacted repeatedly to the same message Gmail limits reactions after more than 20 reactions to the same message Use a normal reply instead of adding more reactions

Why a Gmail reaction can arrive as a separate email

A Gmail reaction is not guaranteed to appear as a neat reaction chip for every recipient. Google says a reaction may look different or arrive as an email that says someone “reacted via Gmail” in several situations.

The recipient uses another email provider

Apple Mail, Microsoft Outlook, or another provider may show the reaction as a separate email instead of the Gmail reaction interface.

The recipient uses an older Gmail app

Updating the Gmail app can restore the newer reaction presentation when the older app version does not support it correctly.

Conversation view is turned off

Gmail can present the reaction differently when messages are not grouped in conversation view.

The recipient does not use a Gmail address

The reaction can be delivered in a form that behaves more like an email notification than an in-thread Gmail reaction.

This is why a reaction can look perfectly normal to the sender but confusing to the recipient. If you need a response to remain clear across email providers, a short text reply is more predictable than a reaction alone.

If the recipient is using Outlook, also see Outlook Emoji Shortcut: Windows, Mac, and Web for the difference between email text emoji and Outlook-specific reactions.

Reaction or normal reply? Use this decision rule

Situation Reaction is usually enough Written reply is safer
Simple acknowledgement When everyone understands what the reaction means When acknowledgement must be recorded clearly in words
Approval or decision Only if the process explicitly accepts reactions When “approved,” “declined,” or conditions need to be unambiguous
Deadline or task assignment Rarely State the date, owner, and next action in text
Casual congratulations or thanks Often reasonable Use text when the relationship or tone is uncertain
Sensitive or corrective message Avoid relying on a reaction alone Write the intended meaning directly

The goal is not to avoid emoji. It is to keep the meaning recoverable if the reaction is displayed differently, forwarded, or read outside Gmail.

A practical recovery sequence when Gmail reactions do not work

  1. Confirm that you are using Gmail itself, not Outlook, Apple Mail, or another email client.
  2. Check whether the problem affects every message or only one message.
  3. If it is one message, check for a group list, more than 20 recipients, BCC, alias, custom reply-to address, or client-side encryption.
  4. If it is a work or school account, consider whether the Workspace administrator disabled reactions.
  5. If reactions arrive as separate emails, check the recipient's email provider, Gmail app version, and conversation-view setting.
  6. If you reacted accidentally, use Undo immediately while the option is still visible.
  7. If the undo window has passed and the reaction could be misunderstood, send a short text clarification.

This sequence avoids treating every reaction problem as an app bug. In many cases, Gmail is working as designed and the correct fix is to identify the message or account condition that disables or changes reactions.
