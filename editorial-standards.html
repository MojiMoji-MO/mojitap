Editorial and Source Standards

Mojitap is maintained by Bavum, Builder and Editor at Mojitap. This page explains how the site's practical emoji guides are researched and revised. It does not claim that every device or third-party app has been personally tested. The goal is to separate documented facts, local browser checks, illustrative examples, and limitations so a reader can tell what supports an instruction.

1. Sources are chosen for the claim being made

For Unicode characters, sequences, presentation selectors, and emoji-version facts, Mojitap prefers Unicode Consortium specifications, charts, and data. For a platform setting or shortcut, the preferred source is documentation from the platform owner, such as Apple, Google, Microsoft, Samsung, or the relevant application provider.

  • Primary technical source: Unicode specifications or the vendor that controls the feature.
  • Secondary explanation: used only when it adds context and does not replace the primary source for a changeable setting.
  • Mojitap examples: written to explain a symptom, decision, or troubleshooting order. They are not presented as customer results.

Useful starting references include the Unicode Emoji charts and the Unicode Emoji FAQ.

2. Documentation and testing are labeled differently

If a guide describes a setting from official documentation, it is treated as a documentation-based instruction unless the page identifies a particular test environment. A browser check on Mojitap can confirm Mojitap's own JavaScript behavior, but it does not prove how every iPhone, Android keyboard, desktop application, or third-party service behaves.

When two platform versions can differ, the guide should tell the reader what control or symptom to look for instead of pretending that one menu path is universal.

3. Examples do not become performance claims

Sample captions, messages, calculations, and comparison tables are illustrative unless a page explicitly identifies a measured source. Mojitap does not turn invented numbers into click-through, revenue, conversion, retention, or ranking claims. If a guide contains arithmetic examples, the page should say that the numbers are examples rather than Mojitap customer data.

4. AI-assisted drafting is disclosed and manually reviewed

AI-assisted drafting and coding tools may be used while creating or revising this site. They are not the source of truth for changeable platform facts. Pages are reviewed against the cited documentation, site behavior, and the scope stated on the page before publication. Text pasted into the homepage emoji editor is processed by the local browser rule engine, not by a language-model service operated by Mojitap.

5. Updates are made for a reason

  • A page is revised when a documented platform control changes, a Unicode version changes, a broken instruction is found, a source becomes outdated, or the explanation can be made materially clearer.
  • Existing URLs are kept when the topic remains the same. Mojitap does not change publication dates merely to make an unchanged article look new.
  • When two old pages are consolidated, the old URL should redirect to the closest useful replacement rather than become a misleading copy.
  • Pages that no longer serve the site's emoji-focused purpose can be removed instead of being kept only to increase page count.

6. Corrections are welcome

If a shortcut, menu path, Unicode sequence, or compatibility statement is wrong, send the page URL and the specific issue through the contact page. For device-specific problems, include the operating system, app or keyboard, and the behavior you see without sending private messages, credentials, or confidential documents.

Advertising does not define the editorial conclusion.
A guide is not rewritten to promise better advertising performance. The site's goal is to explain an emoji task or problem accurately enough that the reader can act on it.
