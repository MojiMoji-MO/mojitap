Why Some Emojis Look Different Across Devices

Unicode defines the character or sequence and its intended identity, but Apple, Google, Microsoft, Samsung, and other platforms create their own artwork. The same emoji can therefore differ in color, detail, or style. Review important messages on the devices and apps used by your audience before publishing.

Why a Flag May Display as Letters or a Black Flag

Most country and territory flags use paired regional indicator characters. England, Scotland, and Wales use longer subdivision sequences. Older software may not support every flag sequence, so a flag can fall back to letters or a generic black flag. That is a device-support limitation rather than a broken link or corrupted page.

Practical and Accessible Emoji Use

Emojis can add tone and visual structure, but they should not replace essential words, instructions, warnings, or link labels. Screen readers may announce an emoji by its Unicode name, and repeated emojis can create noisy output. Use a small number and keep the surrounding sentence understandable without them.
