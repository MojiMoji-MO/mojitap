How to Type Emojis on Windows, Mac, iPhone, and Android

Written by Bavum, Builder and Editor at Mojitap · Published July 22, 2026 · Reviewed against current official platform instructions · About 9 minutes

You do not need a separate emoji app for most everyday writing. Windows, macOS, iPhone, and Android all include a built-in emoji picker or keyboard. The fastest method depends on the device, but the basic workflow is the same: place the cursor in a text field, open the emoji panel, search or browse, and select the character you want.

This guide gives you the shortest reliable method for each major platform, followed by fixes for the problems people encounter most often: a missing emoji key, an emoji that appears as an empty box, a symbol that pastes as a sticker, or a picker that will not open.

Quick answer: the four fastest methods

Windows 11 or Windows 10 Press Windows key + . while the cursor is in a text field.
Mac Press Fn / Globe + E, or choose Edit > Emoji & Symbols .
iPhone Tap the emoji face or globe key, search or browse, then tap an emoji.
Android with Gboard Tap a text field, tap the emoji key, then select one or more emojis.

1. How to type emojis on Windows

Windows includes an emoji panel that works in common text fields, browsers, documents, email clients, and messaging apps. You do not need to copy an emoji from another website every time.

  1. Click inside the place where you want the emoji to appear.
  2. Press Windows key + . (the period key).
  3. Choose an emoji with the mouse, or continue typing a word such as heart, party, or coffee to search.
  4. Select the emoji. It is inserted at the cursor position.

The Windows panel also includes symbols, kaomoji, and—depending on your version and region—other media options. For regular writing, the emoji search is usually faster than browsing through every category.

Practical shortcut: Open the panel only after placing the cursor in an editable field. If the shortcut appears to do nothing, click inside the message, document, or search box and try again.

2. How to type emojis on a Mac

On current macOS versions, the Character Viewer is the main place to search for emojis, symbols, accented letters, and other characters.

  1. Place the cursor in a text field.
  2. Press Fn / Globe + E. You can also open the app's Edit menu and choose Emoji & Symbols .
  3. Search for an emoji by name, or browse a category.
  4. Click the emoji to insert it. If the expanded Character Viewer is open, you may need to double-click.

To choose a skin-tone variation, click and hold an emoji that supports variations. If the Globe or Fn key performs a different action on your Mac, check System Settings > Keyboard and review the setting for what that key should do.

Do not confuse an emoji with a sticker. A normal Unicode emoji behaves like text and can be copied with the sentence. Stickers and custom images may be sent as separate graphics and may not work in documents, forms, or subject lines.

3. How to type emojis on an iPhone

The iPhone emoji keyboard is available in apps that use the standard keyboard, including Messages, Mail, Notes, browsers, and many third-party apps.

  1. Tap a text field to open the keyboard.
  2. Tap the emoji face key. If you use several keyboards, you may see a globe key instead; tap or hold it and switch to Emoji.
  3. Use the search field, swipe through the list, or tap a category icon.
  4. Tap an emoji to insert it. Touch and hold supported people or hand emojis to choose a skin tone.
  5. Tap ABC to return to letters.

If the iPhone emoji keyboard is missing

Open Settings > General > Keyboard > Keyboards > Add New Keyboard , then select Emoji. Return to the app and switch keyboards again.

Predictive suggestions can sometimes offer an emoji while you type, but they are optional. Searching the emoji keyboard directly is more predictable when you need a specific symbol.

4. How to type emojis on Android

Android phones can use different keyboard apps. The steps below apply to Google's Gboard, which is common on Pixel phones and can also be installed on many other Android devices. Samsung Keyboard and other keyboards may place the button in a different position.

  1. Open an app where you can type, such as Messages, Gmail, Keep, or a browser.
  2. Tap the text field to open the keyboard.
  3. Tap the emoji face key.
  4. Tap one or more emojis to insert them.
  5. Tap ABC to return to letters.

If Gboard does not show a separate emoji key

Open Gboard settings, choose Preferences, and review the option for the emoji switch key. On many Gboard layouts, you can reach settings by touching and holding the comma key and sliding to the settings icon. The exact wording can vary by Gboard version and device manufacturer.

Keyboard layouts vary on Android. A Samsung, Pixel, Motorola, or Xiaomi phone may not show identical buttons. The safe approach is to look for the smiley-face icon, a toolbar button, or the keyboard's settings menu rather than assuming every Android screen is the same.

5. The fastest method when you do not know the emoji's name

Built-in search works well when you know a useful keyword. The difficult case is when you remember the appearance but not the official name. A practical process is:

  1. Search a broad idea such as happy, work, food, travel, or warning.
  2. Check nearby emojis in the same category.
  3. Copy the one you need and paste it into your message.
  4. Read the finished sentence once to confirm that the symbol matches the intended tone.

6. Why an emoji may look different after you send it

Most standard emojis are text characters, but Apple, Google, Microsoft, Samsung, and individual apps draw their own artwork. A smile, hand gesture, food item, or flag can therefore look slightly different on another person's device even when the underlying character is the same.

7. A simple rule for clear emoji use

An emoji works best when it supports the words instead of carrying the entire meaning. This matters in work messages, customer support, public posts, and any situation where the reader may interpret tone differently.

Clear: “The file is ready for review ✅”

Less clear: “✅📁👀”

The first version remains understandable if the emoji is displayed differently, read aloud by assistive technology, or removed during copying.

Keep essential dates, prices, warnings, addresses, and instructions in words. Use emojis as visual support, not as replacements for information the reader must understand.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use these methods in a web browser?

Yes. Place the cursor in a form, social post, email, document, or search field, then open the emoji picker for your device. Some secure fields and specialized apps may limit what can be entered.

Why can another person not see the same emoji?

The recipient may be using an older operating system, an app with limited character support, or a platform with different artwork. Updating the device or choosing an older, widely supported emoji often resolves the issue.

Should I install a third-party emoji keyboard?

Usually not for basic use. Built-in keyboards cover common emojis. If you install another keyboard, review its developer, permissions, privacy policy, and update history because keyboards can interact with sensitive text entry.

Is copying an emoji different from typing it?

For a standard Unicode emoji, the result is generally the same character. Copy-and-paste is useful when you cannot find a name or when the built-in picker is inconvenient.
