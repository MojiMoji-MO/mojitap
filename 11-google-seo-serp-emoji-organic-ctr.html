Can Emojis Appear in Google Search Titles and Snippets?

Written by Bavum, Builder and Editor at Mojitap · Originally published June 2026 · Updated August 7, 2026

Emojis can appear in page titles and descriptions, but site owners cannot force Google to display them. Google automatically creates title links and snippets from several page signals and may show different text when it better represents the page or query.

Practical answer

Use an emoji in a title or meta description only when it accurately identifies the subject. Do not depend on it for ranking, click-through rate, or a specific appearance in search.

Know the parts of a search result

PartPrimary sourceWhat the site owner controls
Title linkTitle element, visible headings, prominent text, links, and other signalsYou provide descriptive signals; Google chooses the displayed title.
SnippetUsually page content, sometimes the meta descriptionYou can write a useful description, but it may not be used for every query.
URL or breadcrumbPage URL and site structureUse readable, stable paths rather than decorative characters.

Keep the title concise and descriptive

Google recommends unique, descriptive title text. A symbol at the beginning can push the subject away from the most visible position, especially when the title is long. Match the title element, H1, and visible page topic instead of creating a search-only headline.

Expect Google to rewrite title links

Google's title-link system is automated and can use the title element, main heading, prominent text, anchor text, and other sources. If those elements describe different topics, the displayed title may not match the title element. Updating the page also requires recrawling and reprocessing, which can take time.

Write the meta description as a useful summary

A meta description is a suggestion, not a guaranteed snippet. Google primarily creates snippets from page content and may select a passage that better matches the query. Write one or two accurate sentences instead of a list of keywords and emojis.

Do not put emojis in the URL for appearance

Use simple words and hyphens in URL paths. Decorative characters can make a path harder to type, share, debug, and maintain. The page title and content are better places to describe the topic.

How to diagnose a rewritten result

  1. Compare the title element, H1, Open Graph title, and visible page topic.
  2. Remove vague branding, repeated keywords, and unrelated prominent text.
  3. Write a concise title that matches the page's actual answer.
  4. Request indexing only after the public source contains the final version.
  5. Allow time for Google to recrawl and process the change.

No display guarantee: An emoji that appears in your HTML may be omitted from the search result. Its presence or absence is not evidence that the page is indexed incorrectly.
